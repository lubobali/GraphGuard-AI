# Presenting AML model performance to managers, and what model-risk rules and regulators expect

Research notes, 2026-11-22. Scope: how to show "how good is the model" in GraphGuard's dashboard and demo. GraphGuard facts used here come from this repo, not the web: synthetic IBM AMLworld data, about 0.1% laundering, XGBoost ranking alerts, precision@k as the main metric, 127/168 rings and 542 laundering transactions in the top 5,000 validation alerts against 21 for rules, and labels that arrive late.

Source quality: most industry figures (cost per alert, false-positive rate, SAR conversion) come from **vendors selling alert-reduction tools**, and they are flagged as such below. I found **no regulator-published benchmark** for cost per alert or alert-to-SAR conversion.

---

## 1. How banks and vendors explain detection performance to business owners (metrics and typical figures)

### Takeaway
Business owners think in operations terms: alerts per day the team can work, share of bad activity caught, false-positive share, alert-to-SAR conversion, cost per alert, and lift over the current rules. The usual figures (90-95% false positives from rules, 20-30 minutes and $30-70 per alert, alert-to-SAR under 5% with a 5-15% "good" range) are mostly vendor rules of thumb, not standards. Present them as labelled, adjustable assumptions.

### Cited Findings
- **False-positive rate of rules:** 90-95% is the most repeated figure (often credited to PwC; I could not check the original). It comes from vendors: Sayari says name-matching tools produce 90-95% false positives — [Sayari](https://sayari.com/?p=1777); DataRobot's worked example gives a 90% rules false-positive rate (1436/1600) — [DataRobot](https://www.datarobot.com/use-cases/reduce-false-positives-for-anti-money-laundering-aml/); Fluxforce repeats "PwC 90-95%" — [Fluxforce (vendor)](https://www.fluxforce.ai/statistics/false-positive-rates-transaction-monitoring).
- **Minutes per alert:** about 30 minutes per false alert — [Sayari brief (vendor)](https://sayari.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Sayari_Alert_Adjudication_AMLA_Intelligence_Brief.pdf); 22 minutes on average (Zenoo), and up to 22 hours for a full SAR investigation including documentation and review — reported in [DEV Community (vendor-adjacent)](https://dev.to/stuart_watkins_555e9d30ee/aml-false-positives-cost-compliance-teams-10m-annually-heres-how-to-cut-them-by-80-2fb); 20 minutes per case, which the author calls optimistic — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/why-aml-transaction-monitoring-fails-when-you-optimise-for-volume-instead-of-decisions).
- **Cost per alert:** "$30~$70" to review one alert — [DataRobot docs (vendor)](https://docs.datarobot.com/en/more-info/biz-accelerators/money-launder.html). Aggregate claims: $206bn a year worldwide and over €113M a year per institution (Sayari); £8-15M a year in false-positive cost per institution (Zenoo). These are marketing numbers and not independent — [Sayari](https://sayari.com/?p=2305).
- **Investigator salaries (US, 2025):** Salary.com AML Investigation Analyst average $76,947 (Mar 2025, 10th-90th percentile $60,030-$97,600) — [Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/offering/aml-investigation-analyst-salary); Payscale AML Analyst $58,837 average base — [Payscale](https://payscale.com/research/US/Job=Anti-Money_Laundering_Analyst/Salary); Payscale BSA/AML Analyst $64,725 — [Payscale](https://payscale.com/research/US/Job=Bank_Secrecy_Act_%2F_Anti-Money_Laundering_Analyst/Salary); Salary.com BSA AML Investigator $58,556 — [Salary.com](https://www.salary.com/research/salary/hiring/aml-investigator-ii-salary).
- **Alert-to-SAR conversion:** "typical" under 5%, target 5-15%, credited to "industry consensus, FCA guidance" (no FCA document found) — [Fluxforce (vendor)](https://www.fluxforce.ai/statistics/false-positive-rates-transaction-monitoring); best programmes 8-15%, marked illustrative — [Fluxforce MLRO page (vendor)](https://www.fluxforce.ai/for/mlro-sar-backlog). Protiviti treats both very high and very low conversion as warning signs to investigate, not as goals — [Protiviti TM metrics PoV](https://www.protiviti.com/sites/default/files/2023-03/pov-using-metrics-to-enhance-the-tm-program-protiviti.pdf). Oracle FCCM ships an "Event to SAR Ratio" report as a standard monitoring view — [Oracle docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/financial-services/ofs-analytical-applications/monitor-cloud/fccmmonitor/event-sar-ratio.html). Conversion is usually alert-to-case × case-to-SAR — [DataInterview course](https://www.datainterview.com/courses/risk-fraud-data-science/transaction-monitoring). One real monthly table shows 9,438 alerts, 816 high-risk and 324 SARs (Apr 2023) — [Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/view-large/105971342).
- **Above/below-the-line (ATL/BTL) testing** is how threshold changes are traditionally explained. ATL reviews alerts that fired (would raising the threshold lose anything?). BTL samples just under the threshold to find misses. Example: 5 SARs in 200 BTL reviews (95% interval about 1.1-5.7%), and raising a threshold cut alerts from 141 to 94 while keeping all 10 SARs — [DataInterview](https://www.datainterview.com/courses/risk-fraud-data-science/aml-scenario-tuning); [Sardine BTL (vendor)](https://www.sardine.ai/learn/below-the-line-testing); [Sardine ATL](https://www.sardine.ai/learn/above-the-line-testing). One requirements doc ties BTL sample size to OCC-style sampling (95% confidence, 5% error, n≈384) — [dhilen.github.io](https://dhilen.github.io/BRD).
- **FATF** names a higher "conversion rate of alerts into suspicious activity reports" and fewer false positives as the main promise of ML in monitoring — [FATF handout, Opportunities and Challenges of New Technologies for AML/CFT, July 2021](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/brochures/opportunities-and-challenges-of-new-technologies-handout.pdf).

### Inferences
- GraphGuard's own numbers map straight onto this language. Top 5,000 alerts → 542 laundering transactions is a precision of about 10.8% (about 89% of alerts are not laundering, which is near the "industry" false-positive level but on a far smaller and ranked queue). The rules catch 21 in the same budget, so the lift is about 26×. A manager will grasp "at the same workload, 26× more laundering found, and 127 of 168 rings touched" faster than PR-AUC.
- Workload framing: at the default assumptions (20-30 min per alert, $30-70 per alert), 5,000 alerts is about 1,700-2,500 analyst hours and $150k-$350k. Show these as **sliders with the source next to them**, not as facts.
- Precision@k is the alert-to-case conversion of the top-k queue. Calling it "share of alerts that are real" (with the caveat that real labels are synthetic) links it to SAR conversion, the KPI managers already know.
- Do not claim "value of laundering detected" in dollars as a business benefit. The amounts are synthetic, and no source gives a defensible dollars-per-SAR value. Show volume caught as a count with a note instead.

### Gaps
- No regulator or ACAMS-published benchmark for cost per alert, minutes per alert or SAR conversion was found. The ACAMS webinar page found ([ACAMS](https://www.acams.org/de/training/webinars/transforming-transaction-monitoring-taking-a-tm-program-from-%E2%80%98good%E2%80%99-to-%E2%80%98great%E2%80%99)) had no figures.
- The original PwC 90-95% false-positive source was not retrieved.
- No published "capacity curve" (alerts per day vs % caught) from a named bank was found. It is a standard analytic idea, but the examples are vendor and course material.

## 2. Visualizing threshold and capacity trade-offs for non-technical users

### Takeaway
Research on lay readers says the standard confusion matrix confuses them (which way to read it, what the terms mean). Flow charts and bar charts with natural-frequency counts ("out of 5,000 alerts, 542 are laundering") work better. Metrics alone are not enough: annotate with concrete use cases.

### Cited Findings
- Shen et al., CSCW 2020 (7 interviews, a 102-person survey, a 483-person experiment): lay users struggled with confusion-matrix terms and layout (reading direction, how the layered quantities relate). Redesigned versions helped. The flow chart was most preferred, then a bar chart — [CMU paper](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/hongs/files/CM_CSCW2020.pdf); [project page](https://dig.cmu.edu/publications/2020-confusion.html).
- Natural frequencies (counts, not percentages) reduce base-rate neglect even among CS students reading ML performance — [arXiv 2209.08312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.08312). Gigerenzer's finding that natural frequencies beat probabilities, and that a reference class must always be given, is cited for NLG explanations — [INLG 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.inlg-main.4.pdf).
- Complex metrics derived from the confusion matrix can overwhelm and mislead non-experts — [Beauxis-Aussalet & Hardman, VIS 2014 poster](https://vis.cs.ucdavis.edu/vis2014papers/VIS_Conference/infovis/posters/beauxis-aussalet.pdf).
- Suh et al., "Are Metrics Enough?": accuracy and error metrics do not tell the whole story (risks, strengths, limits). Use visualizations plus annotations and grounded use cases for subject-matter experts — [arXiv 2205.05749](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.05749v3).
- The industry way to explain a threshold is ATL/BTL: show what you would lose by raising it and what you miss below it — see section 1 sources.

### Inferences
- Dashboard pattern for GraphGuard: (1) a **capacity slider** (alerts per day the team can work), driving (2) a plain sentence: "With 5,000 alerts you review X, of which about 542 are laundering and 4,458 are not; you touch 127 of 168 rings; rules at the same workload find 21". (3) A capacity curve (x = alerts worked, y = % of laundering or rings caught) with the rules curve on the same axes, the chosen point marked, and the 0.1% base rate drawn as the "random" line. (4) An optional cost line using labelled assumptions (minutes per alert, hourly cost).
- Avoid PR-AUC and ROC on the manager view. Keep them on a "for the model validator" tab. At a 0.1% base rate the ROC curve flatters the model (the project's own CLAUDE.md rule).
- Show a "missed" count next to "caught" (ring count and transaction count left below the line) so the BTL concept is visible.

### Gaps
- No study was found that tests capacity-slider interfaces specifically for AML or fraud managers. The evidence comes from general lay-audience HCI work.

## 3. Being honest about synthetic data, optimistic validation, label delay and unknown labels; monitoring with late labels

### Takeaway
Supervisory guidance asks you to tell users a model's limits (SR 26-2: "understanding and communicating limitations"). For label delay, the standard tooling is label-free performance estimation (NannyML CBPE, from calibrated probabilities) plus drift monitoring. CBPE fails under concept drift, which is exactly the case in AML, so it must be shown as an estimate with that caveat and later checked against matured labels.

### Cited Findings
- SR 26-2 (Apr 2026): "Users of model output benefit from understanding and communicating limitations, monitoring performance, periodically reviewing relevance, and supplementing model output with complementary analysis." Where a model is used before validation is complete, give "greater attention to the model's limitations… informing relevant stakeholders" and use controls such as "more closely monitoring its performance" — [SR 26-2 attachment](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602a1.pdf).
- NannyML CBPE estimates the confusion matrix, and so any classification metric, from model scores alone when labels are delayed or absent, assuming the probabilities are well calibrated. It handles covariate shift but **not concept drift** — [NannyML repo](https://github.com/NannyML/nannyml); [how it works](https://github.com/NannyML/nannyml/blob/v0.6.0/docs/how_it_works/performance_estimation.rst). The authors later proposed M-CBPE and PAPE for covariate shift — [arXiv 2401.08348](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08348v3).
- A 2026 critique (third party): when the concept changes, calibration degrades and CBPE estimates become systematically biased. It proposes proxy-evidence monitoring for risk systems with delayed ground truth — [arXiv 2604.15740](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15740).
- Protiviti TM metrics: watch volumes, conversion and productivity trends as programme health signals — [Protiviti](https://www.protiviti.com/sites/default/files/2023-03/pov-using-metrics-to-enhance-the-tm-program-protiviti.pdf).
- The AMLA draft asks for stress tests that use "actual performance, past escalations and suspicious activity reports, and emerging threats" — [CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/amla-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-ongoing-monitoring-under-article-26-5-amlr).

### Inferences
- Honesty strip for the demo, always visible: "Synthetic data (IBM AMLworld simulator) · validation window, test window still sealed · labels in live operation arrive 1-60 days late; unlabelled ≠ clean". Matches the project rule "say synthetic first, every time".
- Show three performance layers with distinct visual weight: (a) **measured offline** (validation, solid); (b) **estimated live** (CBPE-style from scores, dashed, with "assumes no concept drift"); (c) **confirmed so far** (matured labels by `labels.known_as_of(t)`, filling in over time, with a "% of alerts still unlabelled" counter). Precision on matured labels is a lower bound while many alerts are unknown (positive-unlabelled). Recall on live data cannot be measured except through BTL sampling, so say so.
- Labels from investigations are biased toward what the champion surfaced (the repo's FINDING-017). Any live precision or recall number should carry that note.
- Leading proxies that need no labels: alert volume per day versus capacity, score distribution drift (PSI or similar), feature drift, the share of cold-start accounts, and the rules-vs-model overlap.

### Gaps
- I found no regulator text addressing positive-unlabelled evaluation in AML specifically.
- I found no published AML case study using CBPE in production.

## 4. Model risk management and regulatory expectations (binding vs guidance), as of Nov 2026

### Takeaway
In the US, SR 11-7 **and** the 2021 BSA/AML model-risk statement (SR 21-8) were replaced on 17 Apr 2026 by SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13. These are non-enforceable, principles-based guidance aimed at banks over $30bn, and they exclude generative and agentic AI but cover classic ML such as XGBoost. The UK's PRA SS1/23 has been in force since 17 May 2024 (internal-model banks). In the EU, the AI Act does not list AML monitoring as high-risk (Annex III targets creditworthiness, with a fraud-detection carve-out), and Annex III duties moved to 2 Dec 2027. The AMLA draft guidelines (June 2026, final expected Q4 2026) want monitoring that is explainable, regularly tested, documented, and validated on material change. Common thread: documentation, validation (conceptual soundness, outcomes analysis, benchmarking), ongoing monitoring, explainability, and showing **effectiveness**, not just owning the technology.

### Cited Findings
**US — SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13 (guidance, not binding)**
- Issued 17 Apr 2026 by the Fed, OCC and FDIC. Supersedes SR 11-7 (2011) and SR 21-8 (2021 interagency BSA/AML model-risk statement) — [Fed SR 26-2 letter](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm). Secondary sources say the OCC side also rescinded OCC 2011-12, 1997-24 and the Comptroller's Handbook model-risk booklet — [RiskTemplate (secondary)](https://risktemplate.com/blog/2026-06-10-occ-bulletin-2026-13-model-risk-management-sr-11-7-what-changed/); [Elevate (secondary)](https://elevateconsult.com/insights/sr-11-7-rescinded-model-risk-guidance-ai-gap/).
- "does not set forth enforceable standards or prescriptive requirements; accordingly, non-compliance with this guidance will not result in supervisory criticism". It also says "supervisory action may result for any violations of law or unsafe or unsound practices". Most relevant to banks over $30bn in assets — [SR 26-2 attachment](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602a1.pdf).
- A model is "a complex quantitative method… that applies statistical, economic, or financial theories to process input data into quantitative estimates". It **excludes** "deterministic rule-based processes". So a rules engine is not a model under this guidance, while an XGBoost scorer is. "Generative AI and agentic AI models… are not within the scope", but the principles apply to "non-generative, non-agentic AI models" — same source.
- Validation components: **conceptual soundness** (design, assumptions, data selection; "interpretability measures or benchmarking to other models" may be more practical than theory), **outcomes analysis** (compare outputs with real outcomes; back-testing, outlier analysis; act on "persistent deviations outside… established performance thresholds"), and **ongoing monitoring** (is it "performing as expected given potential changes in products, exposures, activities, clients, data relevance"; frequency depends on materiality). Also: model inventory, documentation, clear roles, managing developer/validator conflicts of interest, and validation plus ongoing monitoring of vendor models — same source.

**US — FinCEN (proposed rule, not final)**
- An AML/CFT Program NPRM was published Apr 2026 (comments closed 9 Jun 2026). It moves to an effectiveness, risk-based framework. The preamble discusses ML and generative AI, and FinCEN may weigh the use of innovative tools "that demonstrate the effectiveness" of a programme when deciding enforcement. This is encouraged, not mandated, with a proposed 12-month implementation after the final rule — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/FinCEN-Proposes-Rule-to-Modernize-AML-CFT-Program-Requirements); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/sitecore/content/whlaunch/home/research/news-publications/20260413-fincen-issues-proposed-rule-revising-the-aml-program-requirements-for-financial-institutions); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-sa/insights/publications/2026/04/treasury-proposes-fundamental-reform-of-financial-institutions-aml-cft-program-requirements). I did not retrieve the Federal Register text itself.

**UK — PRA SS1/23 (supervisory statement; expectations for in-scope firms)**
- Five principles, in force 17 May 2024. Scope narrowed to internal-model banks. It covers AI/ML "to the extent that it applies to the use of models more generally" — [Bank of England SS1/23](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2023/may/model-risk-management-principles-for-banks-ss); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/blogs/ecrs/from-principle-to-practice-model-risk-management-takes-effect.html). A PRA AI roundtable (Oct 2025) restated that it applies to all model types including AI/ML — [BoE roundtable](https://wwwtest.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/publication/2025/november/ai-roundtable-oct-2025.pdf). KPMG (Mar 2026) says SS1/23-based controls may not fully capture AI risks such as data drift — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/regulatory-insights/beyond-compliance.html).

**EU — AI Act (binding regulation)**
- Annex III point 5(b) (creditworthiness) excludes "AI systems used for the purpose of detecting financial fraud". AML transaction monitoring is not named in Annex III — [AI Act Service Desk, Annex III](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/annex-3); [McCann FitzGerald on draft classification guidelines](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/technology-and-innovation/essential-services-spotlight-eu-ai-act-draft-guidelines-on-high-risk-ai-classification). The "AML is not high-risk by default" reading is my interpretation, not a quoted ruling.
- The Digital Omnibus on AI was adopted (Parliament 16 Jun 2026, Council 29 Jun 2026, signed 8 Jul 2026). Annex III high-risk duties apply from **2 Dec 2027**, Annex I from 2 Aug 2028 — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/EU-AI-Act-Update-Digital-Omnibus-Finalizes-8-Compliance-Changes); [Lewis Silkin](https://lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/09/council-of-the-eu-gives-ai-omnibus-final-green-light-102nbb1).

**EU — AMLR / AMLA (AMLR binding from 2027-28; guidelines in draft)**
- AMLA consultation on draft guidelines for ongoing monitoring under Art. 26(5) AMLR: published 3 Jun 2026, comments by 3 Sep 2026, final expected Q4 2026. Monitoring (manual, automated or semi-automated) must be "explainable", "regularly tested" and "properly documented". Firms should assess whether ML/AI would improve detection, but "Simply having such technology in place is not, on its own, proof that the framework works well". They must be able to explain AI tools' "role, functioning, and outputs" to authorities. Material changes to monitoring logic need "a documented transition and validation framework". Automated closure is allowed only where automated analysis finds nothing suspicious — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/amla-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-ongoing-monitoring-under-article-26-5-amlr); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/financial-services/1842880/amla-publishes-draft-guidelines-on-ongoing-monitoring-under-article-265-amlr). Commentary says AMLA becomes fully operational in 2027 and the AMLR applies from (July) 2027, with direct supervision from 2028 — [Fintech Global (secondary)](https://fintech.global/2026/04/02/how-amla-is-reshaping-financial-crime-compliance/). I did not open AMLA's own consultation paper.

**FATF (international standard-setter; report = guidance)**
- July 2021 report: ML is the most promising technology. It can cut false positives, find complex cases and raise alert-to-SAR conversion. Challenges: cost, staff skills, legacy systems, privacy, and lack of explainability and transparency. Supervisors should understand the models, and firms remain responsible for the technical details before deployment — [FATF handout](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/brochures/opportunities-and-challenges-of-new-technologies-handout.pdf); [Kroll summary](https://www.kroll.com/en/insights/publications/financial-compliance-regulation/fatf-new-technologies-aml-cft); [FATF Digital Transformation page](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/digitaltransformation/digital-transformation.html).

**Wolfsberg Group (industry body; voluntary)**
- Part I (Jul 2024), "Moving Beyond Automated Transaction Monitoring", is about effective outcomes. Part II (27 Aug 2025), "Transitioning to Innovation", gives a responsible transition framework for AI/ML. Its pillars include "explainability to demonstrate transparency in coverage and effectiveness", transition and validation, and balancing model risk against financial-crime risk — [Wolfsberg news](https://wolfsberg-group.org/news/the-wolfsberg-group-publishes-its-second-statement-on-effective-monitoring-for-suspicious-activity). Secondary summaries say it backs a hybrid of rules plus supervised and unsupervised ML, and favours judging new systems on modern performance measures and proof-of-concept/historical testing over long parallel runs against legacy systems — [Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/?p=214048); [Shufti (vendor)](https://shuftipro.com/news/wolfsberg-group-ai-guidance-for-financial-institutions/). Earlier: Wolfsberg "Principles for Using AI and ML in Financial Crime Compliance" (2022) — [Wolfsberg resources](https://wolfsberg-group.org/resources/202/).

### Inferences
- A "model risk card" for GraphGuard maps cleanly onto SR 26-2 and AMLA: purpose and use, data (synthetic, its limits), conceptual soundness (features, leakage contract, ADRs), benchmarking (rules, dumb baselines, the GNN as challenger), outcomes analysis (validation precision@k and ring recall; the sealed test window), ongoing monitoring plan (drift, CBPE-style estimate, matured-label backtest, BTL sampling), limitations, explainability (per-alert reasons), change/transition validation (champion/challenger shadow), and inventory metadata (version, hash, owner, validator).
- Because SR 26-2 excludes deterministic rules from "model" but includes XGBoost, the dashboard's "model vs rules" comparison is also the regulatory story: the model takes on MRM duties the rules did not have, and the card is how you pay that.
- The investigator agent planned for Phase 7, if LLM-based, falls under SR 26-2's generative/agentic exclusion. It is governed by general risk management, not the MRM guidance. Say so on the card.
- Use "effectiveness" wording (FinCEN NPRM, AMLA, Wolfsberg): caught at fixed capacity and conversion, not AUC.
- State clearly in the demo that this is a portfolio project and not a bank-validated model. "Validation" in the ML sense is not "independent model validation" in the SR 26-2 sense.

### Gaps
- Full text of Wolfsberg Part II and AMLA's own consultation paper not read (secondary summaries only).
- Not checked: OCC Bulletin 2026-13 text, the EBA's position on ML in AML monitoring, and any FCA-specific guidance.
- No 2026 FATF update on new technologies for AML was found in this pass.
