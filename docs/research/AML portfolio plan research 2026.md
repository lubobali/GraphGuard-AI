# Keep the plan, sharpen four parts

**Short answer: yes, the GraphGuard-AI plan is still current in November 2026, and on two of the five questions it is more current than when it was written.** The business problem is real, and regulators now pay attention to it: since October 2024 a UK *receiving* bank pays half of every APP scam refund, and in September 2026 the FCA told firms to look past the first receiving account to the linked accounts behind it. That is a ring-level mule queue, which is what this project builds. The main technical result (a strict point-in-time XGBoost beats the GNN) now has outside support: a November 2026 preprint found the same thing on the same benchmark. But four parts need changing. First, the GNN that lost is too weak (vanilla GraphSAGE), so a reviewer will ask "why not Multi-PNA?" Second, the numbers are in a different metric from the literature (PR-AUC vs minority-class F1), so nobody can place them. Third, Redpanda changed its licence this month and should be swapped for Kafka. Fourth, the plan never tests the pipeline on a second dataset. Hiring postings ask for capabilities, not tool names. For a career-switcher, the best path in is through serving and platform roles, so the project should lead with its serving and operations half and its evaluation rigour, and not with how many tools it uses. The synthetic-data caveat is still unavoidable: no public bank-transfer AML dataset with real labels exists.

## Receiving-bank mule detection is now a cost banks carry

The plan's problem framing is stronger in late 2026 than it was a year ago. UK APP scam losses reached **£576.4m in 2025, up 19%**, with investment scams up 40% ([UK Finance](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/news-and-insight/press-release/fraud-report-2026-press-release)). Since 7 October 2024 the sending and receiving banks **split each refund 50/50, up to £85,000 per claim** ([Bratby Law on the PSR evaluation](https://bratby.law/app-fraud-reimbursement-evaluation/)). Before that rule, a bank that hosted a mule account paid nothing. Now every scam payment that lands in a mule account costs that bank half the refund. The first independent evaluation found in-scope losses fell about 21%, but fraud moved to international payments and crypto, which are outside the rule's scope ([Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/app-fraud-reimbursement-evaluation/)).

The FCA's September 2026 mule review gives the clearest match to this project. UK firms closed **238,396 suspected mule accounts in 2025** but reported only 15.3% of them to the shared Cifas database. Cash-out usually happens **between the 2nd and 5th account in a chain**. The FCA expects controls to look "beyond the initial receiving account" at linked accounts and transaction context ([Zyphe summary of the FCA review](https://www.zyphe.com/resources/news/fca-money-mule-accounts-review-september-2026); [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/multi-firm-reviews/money-mules-activity-cashing-out-findings)). A ranked ring queue is that idea turned into a product.

Enforcement points the same way, though less directly. Regulators fine banks for monitoring that is *missing or backlogged*, not only for being noisy. TD Bank left about 92% of its transaction volume unmonitored and paid about $3.09bn ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/10/10/td-bank-3-billion-fine-doj-settle-money-laundering-drug-cartel.html)). At Cash App, an alert backlog meant some SARs were filed more than a year late, and NYDFS fined it $40m ([GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/nydfs-hits-block-inc-with-40-million-settlement-over-cash-app-compliance-failures)). Precision@k at a fixed investigator capacity is the honest way to talk about backlogs, and the capacity slider makes the idea easy to see. One warning for the write-up: the popular "90-95% of alerts are false positives" figure has **no primary source** that the research could find ([FluxForce, vendor](https://www.fluxforce.ai/statistics/false-positive-rates-transaction-monitoring)), so do not quote it as fact.

Three honesty points belong in the write-up. AMLworld simulates laundering in general, not APP scams. Its data has no "victim pays mule" link. And a real bank sees only its own slice of a ring, never the full graph the simulator gives you.

## Postings ask for proof of ownership, not tool names

The research read the full text of **58 live fraud/AML/risk ML postings from 29 employers**, collected on 17 November 2026. The pattern is clear. **93% use production/deployment language, 59% ask for monitoring and drift, and 50% ask for real-time or low-latency work** (71% of engineer roles). Graph methods show up in 40%, but GNNs by name in only 7%. The platform tools in this plan almost never appear by name: **Feast, Tecton and Chalk 0 of 58, Ray Serve 0, Kafka 1, MLflow 2, Kubernetes 4, SHAP 0** (all counts from the posting table in the research notes, each linked to the employer's own job page, e.g. [Stripe Radar](https://stripe.com/jobs/search?gh_jid=7983456), [Sardine MLE](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/sardine/2c8f0342-b8af-4cc2-85cd-67bc48275568)). So the tools in this plan help as *evidence of a capability*: train/serve consistency, a latency budget, streaming replay, drift and retraining. They do not help as keywords to match.

Some requirements line up almost word for word with what this project already does. Plaid requires experience with "label quality, data leakage, class imbalance, and generalization across time periods" ([Plaid Sr MLE Fraud](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/plaid/e9a9aeab-be90-43f3-b898-63a846cb5777)). Stripe wants "deep foundations in… model evaluation" and a project taken "from problem definition to production" ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/jobs/search?gh_jid=7983456)). The leakage contract and the honest GNN result are exactly this kind of evidence.

The hard part is seniority. **41 of 58 titles are Senior or above, and the median minimum is 6 years.** The best opening for someone coming from AI-application engineering is Sardine's MLE posting: "Experience building, not just using, model serving infrastructure… **You don't need to have trained one recently**, but when precision drops you should know the difference between a data problem, a feature pipeline problem, and a model problem" ([Sardine](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/sardine/2c8f0342-b8af-4cc2-85cd-67bc48275568)). Coinbase's ML platform role counts software-engineering years ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/careers/positions/8211490?gh_jid=8211490)), and Plaid counts "software engineering for ML" toward its 7 years ([Plaid](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/plaid/e9a9aeab-be90-43f3-b898-63a846cb5777)). Coinbase's compliance-automation role wants people who have shipped LLM and agent applications ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/careers/positions/8333165?gh_jid=8333165)), which plays directly to the owner's background. These roles weight Phases 5, 6 and 8 (serving, stream, monitoring) more than Phase 7's UI polish. Champion/challenger appears in only **2 of 58 postings, both in credit**, so it is worth building but should stay small.

## The tree-wins result is current, but the losing GNN is too weak

On the benchmark itself, published HI-Small results use **minority-class F1, not PR-AUC**. Tuned trees on graph features score about 0.63, Multi-PNA 0.68, and **MEGA-PNA and Multi-FraudGT 0.74-0.76** ([MEGA-GNN, TMLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00241v3)). A November 2026 preprint, arXiv 2611.18914, re-checked this benchmark. It found that the standard Multi-GNN code builds the test graph **from all transactions, including ones after the transaction being scored**, and samples neighbours without regard to time. A strictly point-in-time LightGBM scored **F1 0.666 ± 0.045**, level with the best published GNNs ([arXiv 2611.18914](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18914)). That is independent support for GraphGuard's finding. Treat it with care, though: it is a preprint, and the authors disclose a commercial interest.

The same preprint found a simulator artifact: after day 10, normal activity stops and only laundering continues, so "flag every transfer on day 10 or later" alone scores F1 0.482 ([arXiv 2611.18914](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18914)). **This does not affect GraphGuard.** The frozen split is train Sep 1-6, validation Sep 6-8, test Sep 8-10, and `docs/findings.md` already documents and excludes the post-Sep-10 tail. The write-up should say this outright, because it shows the project found the artifact on its own before the preprint came out.

The weak spot is the opponent. GraphSAGE at 0.045 against XGBoost at 0.28 is a much bigger gap than anything published. A senior reviewer will say "you picked a weak GNN," because Multi-PNA and MEGA handle multi-edges, direction and edge attributes and GraphSAGE does not. IBM's Multi-GNN code is public ([IBM/Multi-GNN](https://github.com/IBM/Multi-GNN)). Losing or tying with Multi-PNA *under a strict protocol* is a real result. Losing with GraphSAGE is easy to dismiss. There is also one direct counter-claim to engage with: a September 2026 preprint reports GATv2 at **AUPRC 64.85% against XGBoost 38.46%** on a "leakage-safe" HI-Small split ([arXiv 2609.37155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37155)). Only its abstract was read, so its test-graph construction is unknown. The write-up should cite it and say exactly which protocol detail would explain the difference.

Two other trends matter. Payments "foundation models" (Stripe, Nubank, Visa) are the big 2025-26 production story, but they work at tens of billions of events and act as **feature providers into existing models**, not replacements ([TechCrunch on Stripe](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/07/stripe-unveils-ai-foundation-model-for-payments-reveals-deeper-partnership-with-nvidia); [Visa](https://usa.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/regional/na/us/Solutions/documents/large-transaction-models.pdf)). One paragraph saying why they are out of scope at 5M synthetic rows is enough. On LLM case summaries, the best-known research system reports **no quantitative metrics at all** ([Co-Investigator AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08380)), and vendor results such as FIS's "90%+ faster review" are projections ([FIS](https://www.fisglobal.com/about-us/media-room/press-release/2026/fis-aml-investigations-agent-now-commercially-available)). Because the public bar is so low, a summary that is *measured* stands out. Every claim should cite a transaction ID, a deterministic checker should confirm each ID and amount, and the error rate should be scored against AMLworld's labelled typologies. Governance points the same way. US model-risk guidance SR 26-2 (April 2026) covers ML scoring models but leaves generative and agentic AI out ([Tyche Consulting](https://www.tycheconsulting.com/insights/april-2026-mrm-update-and-ai.html)). So keep the tree and SHAP as the decision, and keep the LLM as an optional layer on top that never makes the decision.

## AMLworld is still the benchmark; TransXion is the second check

**AMLworld is still the reference dataset**, with 184 citations, and every new benchmark paper defines itself against it ([Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2306.16424); [AMLworld paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.16424)). No public bank-transfer AML dataset has real labels. The only real labelled laundering data is on Bitcoin (Elliptic), which is a different task ([Elliptic2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19109)). So "synthetic, said first" stays the rule.

The new critique is that AMLworld injects a fixed set of template patterns, so a model that scores well may just be recognising templates. TransXion (KDD '26) makes this argument and releases a dataset from a different, LLM-agent generator: **3.03M transactions, 47.5K accounts, 0.153% laundering, and the exact same 11-column CSV header as AMLworld**, verified by download ([TransXion paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17420v2); [repo](https://github.com/chaos-max/TransXion)). The authors' own XGBoost AP falls from 0.376 on AMLworld to 0.172 on TransXion. The file is 245MB, half the size of HI-Small, so it fits the box. It has limits. It has no ring file, so "rings caught at k" does not carry over. 95.6% of its rows are in one currency (CNY). And its data has **no explicit licence**, so use it but do not re-host it. Even so, "same pipeline, retuned on a second, independently generated synthetic benchmark" is the cheapest strong robustness claim the project can make. It also answers the template critique directly.

Phase 9 (HI-Large, 180M rows over 97 days) is a weaker use of time. By simple scaling from HI-Small's 476MB, the raw CSV would be roughly 17GB, which is most of the 25GB disk budget. HI-Large is also the same generator, so it tests scale, not generalisation. Keep it optional and put it after TransXion.

## The stack fits, except Redpanda and possibly Ray

The box is already tight. k3s idles at about 953MB and MLflow at about 695MB, with Postgres capped at 512M, so about **2GB of the 4GB budget is used before Ray, a stream broker or Airflow** (measured figures from the project's own CLAUDE.md). That changes some of the choices.

**Redpanda should be replaced.** From version 26.3.1 (shipping 20 November 2026), Redpanda's community edition is no longer source-available. It is a proprietary binary, free up to 50 cores ([Redpanda R2 announcement](https://www.redpanda.com/blog/r2-the-engine-of-engines)). Kafka 4.x runs without ZooKeeper (KRaft only) and is Apache 2.0 ([Apache Kafka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_Kafka)), so the old "Redpanda is lighter" argument is weaker. "Kafka" is also the one streaming name that appears in a posting ([Sift](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/sift/45b22605-1abb-483e-8ef7-5ceaf04f5868)). Kafka's single-node RAM was not measured, so cap it and measure it.

**Ray Serve stays only if it fits and stays private.** It is already deployed (commit 9caa67a), so the question is footprint, not feasibility. Ray had a DNS-rebinding RCE (CVE-2025-62593, fixed in 2.52.0) added to CISA's actively-exploited list in 2026, and "ShadowRay 2.0" turns unpatched clusters into crypto miners ([sota.io](https://sota.io/blog/ray-cve-2025-62593-cisa-kev-shadowray-cra-article-14-2026); [Penligent](https://www.penligent.ai/hackinglabs/the-zombie-vulnerability-a-2026-autopsy-of-cve-2023-48022-and-the-shadowray-2-0-resurgence/)). For one XGBoost model, Ray adds recognition, not capability. If the measured head + replica memory pushes the total past 4GB, a FastAPI Deployment on k3s is the honest swap, plus one line in the write-up explaining why.

**Feast: use it as a library only.** Feast is active (0.68.0, 10 November 2026) but had several critical 2026 CVEs, including unauthenticated RCE in its feature and registry servers (CVE-2026-18948, CVSS 9.9) and a JWT bypass through 0.66.0 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/feast/); [SentinelOne](https://www.sentinelone.com/vulnerability-database/cve-2026-18948/); [VulnCheck](https://www.vulncheck.com/advisories/feast-through-0.66.0-authentication-bypass-via-unverified-token)). The project already has its own online store. If Feast is added, pin it to 0.68 or later, use it only for the offline point-in-time join and `materialize` into Redis, and never run its servers. Saying "feature server deliberately not deployed because of these CVEs" shows security judgement. Tecton has been absorbed into Databricks, so Feast is the open-source name people still recognise ([Perficient](https://blogs.perficient.com/2025/09/26/databricks-acquires-tecton/)).

The rest mostly stands. **MLflow 3.18** is very active and now includes LLM tracing and judges, so it can cover the case-summary evaluation with no extra RAM ([MLflow releases](https://github.com/mlflow/mlflow/releases)). Use registry aliases (`@champion`, `@challenger`) for champion/challenger. **Evidently 0.7.23** is still Apache 2.0 and runs as a batch job with zero idle RAM. Write against the new 0.7 API, not `evidently.legacy` ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/evidently/)). **Airflow 3.4** is the industry default ([Airflow](https://airflow.apache.org/announcements/)), but its several processes are heavy next to k3s and MLflow. Run it minimal, with LocalExecutor, a hard cap and the existing Postgres. If it does not fit, swap in Dagster. Only 6 of 58 postings name any orchestrator, so this choice matters less than it seems. For the LLM layer, expose the agent's tools (case lookup, subgraph fetch, score explanation) as an MCP server. MCP is now governed by the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating%2Dthe%2Dmodel%2Dcontext%2Dprotocol%2Dand%2Destablishing%2Dof%2Dthe%2Dagentic%2Dai%2Dfoundation)). Skip Langfuse, because it needs ClickHouse and will not fit. The research did not check NVIDIA NIM's 2026 status, so keep it as planned.

## Conclusion

The project's strongest asset has changed. A year ago it was "a full ML platform on one box." Now it is **"a strict leakage contract that independently reached the same conclusion as the newest benchmark critique."** That is rare. Most AMLworld papers still use the leaky protocol, and hiring postings name leakage and time generalisation as hard requirements. The revisions below protect that asset. They give the tree a fair, strong opponent and report in the literature's metric, so the result cannot be waved away. They add a second dataset, so the result is not tied to one generator. And they keep the stack honest about memory and security.

For the owner's career, the research shifts the emphasis away from breadth. Fraud-ML hiring is senior-heavy. The open door for a switcher is "can build and debug serving, and understands the model enough to know a data problem from a model problem." So the measured parts (p99 under load, train/serve parity, a drift alert followed by a retrain on the replay, a model card, a measured LLM summary error rate) are worth more than any extra tool. The 8b post should lead with the protocol finding, not the tool list.

## Recommended plan revisions

1. **Swap Redpanda for Apache Kafka 4.x (KRaft, single node, hard memory cap) in Phase 6.** Evidence: Redpanda's community edition stops being source-available from 26.3.1 on 20 Nov 2026 ([Redpanda](https://www.redpanda.com/blog/r2-the-engine-of-engines)). Kafka 4.x no longer needs ZooKeeper ([Kafka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_Kafka)). Kafka is the only streaming tool named in the 58 postings. Measure its RAM on the box before committing. **Priority: must.**

2. **Add minority-class F1 (threshold picked on validation) next to PR-AUC and precision@k, and report it on validation now.** Evidence: the whole HI-Small literature (0.63-0.76) uses this metric ([MEGA](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00241v3); [arXiv 2611.18914](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18914)). Without it, nobody can place 0.28 PR-AUC. Keep precision@k as the headline. Run it through `evaluate()`, so the test set stays sealed. **Priority: must.**

3. **Upgrade the research-arm GNN to Multi-PNA (or MEGA-PNA) under the project's strict point-in-time protocol, with the same Optuna trial budget.** Evidence: GraphSAGE is much weaker than published GNNs, and the 0.045 vs 0.28 gap is out of line with every published comparison ([MEGA](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.00241v3)). IBM's code is public ([IBM/Multi-GNN](https://github.com/IBM/Multi-GNN)). Record it as a Plan revision (R-2), not a silent change. **Priority: must** before the 8b write-up.

4. **Write the protocol finding as the headline, citing 2611.18914 as independent support and engaging with 2609.37155 as the counter-claim.** If budget allows, show the effect directly: one GNN run with a full-history test graph and one with a point-in-time graph, both on validation. Evidence: [arXiv 2611.18914](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18914); [arXiv 2609.37155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37155). State plainly that the day-10 tail was already excluded by the frozen split and FINDING-documented before the preprint appeared. **Priority: should.**

5. **Add TransXion as a second-dataset robustness check (a new phase between 8 and 9), retuned on its own validation window.** Evidence: same CSV schema, different generator family, peer-reviewed at KDD '26, 245MB ([TransXion](https://github.com/chaos-max/TransXion)). Drop the ring metric there, re-audit the FINDING-006 artifacts, and do not re-host the data (no data licence). **Priority: should.**

6. **Measure Ray Serve's real memory on k3s and decide: keep it (Ray ≥ 2.52, dashboard ClusterIP only) or replace it with FastAPI on k3s.** Do this together with the open Phase 5 items (p99 on k3s, cold-account test, S3 load). Evidence: CVE-2025-62593 on CISA KEV ([sota.io](https://sota.io/blog/ray-cve-2025-62593-cisa-kev-shadowray-cra-article-14-2026)); 0 of 58 postings name Ray Serve; about 2GB of the budget is already used. **Priority: must** (the measurement), with the outcome left open.

7. **If Feast is added, use it as a library only (≥ 0.68, offline join plus materialise into Redis, no feature or registry server), and document why.** Evidence: CVE-2026-18948 (CVSS 9.9) and the JWT bypass through 0.66.0 ([SentinelOne](https://www.sentinelone.com/vulnerability-database/cve-2026-18948/); [VulnCheck](https://www.vulncheck.com/advisories/feast-through-0.66.0-authentication-bypass-via-unverified-token)). Keeping the current own online store is also fine, since 0 of 58 postings name Feast. **Priority: should.**

8. **Make the LLM case summary measurable: tools over MCP, a transaction-ID citation on every claim, a deterministic ID/amount checker, and an unsupported-claim rate plus typology recall against AMLworld patterns, logged in MLflow.** Evidence: the published research has no metrics ([Co-Investigator AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08380)), and SR 26-2 leaves GenAI outside model-risk guidance ([Tyche](https://www.tycheconsulting.com/insights/april-2026-mrm-update-and-ai.html)), so the LLM must stay advisory. **Priority: should.**

9. **Add a simple rules layer as a baseline and safety net, and model label delay in the Phase 6/8 replay** (labels arrive days after the alert). Evidence: production systems are hybrids of rules, ML and investigators ([Booking.com](https://medium.com/booking-com-development/leverage-graph-technology-for-real-time-fraud-detection-and-prevention-438336076ea5)), and Plaid names "delayed feedback" ([Plaid](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/plaid/e9a9aeab-be90-43f3-b898-63a846cb5777)). **Priority: should.**

10. **Reframe the business story in the README and 8b post as "receiving-bank mule-network detection," with the three honesty caveats** (synthetic, not APP-specific, a real bank sees only its subgraph). Evidence: the UK 50/50 refund split ([Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/app-fraud-reimbursement-evaluation/)) and the FCA's "beyond the first account" expectation ([FCA via Zyphe](https://www.zyphe.com/resources/news/fca-money-mule-accounts-review-september-2026)). Drop any "90-95% false positives" line. **Priority: should.**

11. **Keep Airflow only if a minimal, capped LocalExecutor setup fits beside k3s and MLflow; otherwise use Dagster.** Keep champion/challenger small (MLflow aliases plus a shadow-scoring comparison). Evidence: Airflow 3 runs several processes; only 6/58 postings name an orchestrator and only 2/58 name champion/challenger. **Priority: should.**

12. **Build the Phase 8 model card as a short model-risk package** (purpose, data, limits, validation, monitoring, kill switch). Evidence: 53% of postings use governance language, and JPMorgan asks for "governance and Model Risk Management packages" ([JPM](https://jpmc.fa.oraclecloud.com/hcmUI/CandidateExperience/en/sites/CX_1001/job/210790266)). **Priority: should.**

13. **Write the 8b material to lead with serving/operations numbers and the evaluation story, aimed at platform/serving MLE and LLM-compliance roles.** Evidence: senior-heavy market (median 6 years), with the clearest switcher opening in Sardine's "you don't need to have trained one recently" ([Sardine](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/sardine/2c8f0342-b8af-4cc2-85cd-67bc48275568)) and Coinbase's LLM compliance role ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/careers/positions/8333165?gh_jid=8333165)). **Priority: should.**

14. **Add one paragraph on why transaction foundation models are out of scope (scale), and keep Phase 9 (HI-Large) optional and after TransXion.** Evidence: FM wins are at tens of billions of events ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/07/stripe-unveils-ai-foundation-model-for-payments-reveals-deeper-partnership-with-nvidia)), and HI-Large is the same generator at roughly 17GB raw (estimate) against a 25GB disk budget. **Priority: optional.**

15. **Use Evidently's 0.7 API (not `evidently.legacy`) and back up the MLflow volume before any 3.18 upgrade.** Evidence: [Evidently on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/evidently/); the MLflow 3.18 upgrade needs a `db upgrade` ([MLflow releases](https://github.com/mlflow/mlflow/releases)). **Priority: optional.**

## What not to change

1. **XGBoost stays the production model.** Strict point-in-time trees matching or beating GNNs is now an outside-supported finding ([arXiv 2611.18914](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18914)).
2. **The leakage contract, the frozen split and the sealed test set.** These are the project's rarest asset and match hiring requirements word for word. Do not reopen the split.
3. **Precision@k and the capacity slider as the headline.** They speak the "fixed investigator capacity" language of backlogs and effectiveness.
4. **AMLworld HI-Small as the main dataset, with "synthetic" said first.** It is still the standard benchmark, and no real public alternative exists.
5. **SHAP "why" and the drawn ring in the UI.** Tree explanations are the governance-friendly choice, and the drawn ring matches the FCA's "linked accounts" ask.
6. **MLflow and the existing Ray Serve/k3s work**, unless the memory measurement in revision 6 says otherwise.
7. **The GraphSAGE negative result itself.** Keep it as documented history (FINDING-007). Revision 3 adds a stronger GNN; it does not erase the old one.
8. **One step at a time.** None of these revisions justify running ahead through a phase.
